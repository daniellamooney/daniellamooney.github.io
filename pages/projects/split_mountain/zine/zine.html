Split mountain zine

The idea for this zine was borne out of a desire to understand how people have come to perceive and interact with landscape. In the open call I didn’t want to be prescriptive about what landscape might be, or how to interpret it, and actually the term landscape has never sat comfortably with me, something about the unsullied depictions of nature which belie the deeper tensions and complexities embedded in the land. And so I was more interested in how people might interpret where the bounds of an environment might start and end. Where are the lines drawn, memories shaped, permissions given, physical abilities extended and desires projected, that all shape the ways in which we move through and navigate space. Maybe because of the ’Split Mountain’ title and my own love of mountains, a lot of the pieces submitted contained the strong presence of mountains. It’s been nice to read and see the varied effects these bodies exert, and im grateful to all those who felt the pull to respond and contribute to this collection. At times intensely personal, historical, poetic and formal, a lot of the accounts weave perspectives with long views while also being deeply enmeshed in the substance of one’s immediate surroundings. The scenes framed here speak to the many ways in which we form relationships to place, to land, and landscape, how rocks and trees and plants and sunsets communicate back to us, creating rich personal dialogues and archives.
