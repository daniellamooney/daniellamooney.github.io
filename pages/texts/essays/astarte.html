Glossary of a Stone Suit or: what lies hidden within geological layers
By Lena Astarte Posch

A — Alabaster. Alive. Angels. I am gonna tell a secret story with unsatisfactory human language words. It will only be a glimpse as these letters are meant to be transformed into a sigil. We are circling around something that lies hidden within geological layers.  B — Basalt. Becoming. Body. Being. Was geht in ihm vor? Ein Gesicht schlafend, friedlich. Es runzelt die Stirn. Falten im Gesicht. Projektion einer Melancholie, einer Frage, eines Leidens. Was denkst du? Was dachtest du? Was passierte in den Jahren der Konservation – ein Gesicht preserviert über tausende Jahre. Ein Kopf. Ein toter Kopf, verschlossen im Moor. D painted his face. Or maybe she painted mine. Or maybe she painted something that has no face yet, something that was always already there, waiting to be seen. C — Chalk. Crypt. Cave. Chemicals. I'm gonna tell a story. My father was a stone mason. He earned his money with tombs. Carving name after name into rocks. Day after day, layer after layer. He said he loved it – no one bothered him there. He was alone in silence with rocks. Like they understood him better. The rocks. The grave stones. He told me about grave stones, they didn't fall from the sky. They have a history. People have been using stones for many many years for graves. Making tombs, caves for the deceased. To bury them back in the earth. For renewal? For remembrance? Bodies decay back to earth. What remains in the graves are often objects. Artefacts. Artworks. He was born in 1948 in Dachau. There was a concentration camp in Dachau. And there was a silence. A denial of the cruelties that had happened. My father was often silent, didn't speak much in general except to tell some weird funny stories or explain some historical facts. He read encyclopedias for fun and also books about history, especially German history, about Hitler and the Nazis. He read them again and again. I imagine he was looking for answers, answers that could fill the silence. D — Dolmen. Death. Lanken Granitz. –1476. 0. 2024. D and I made a trip to visit old Dolmen on Rügen. Dolmen are old gravesites from the New Stone Age, often consisting of two erected stones and a large stone slab on top. They were built with glacial boulders, huge stones. 3500 years ago people built tombs with stones. People have always used stones to bury human remains under large pieces of rock. E — Erosion. Extraction. Enclosed. Earth. The day was grey, cold breeze whistling in the leaves of the trees. No one was around. We entered into another world, another time. Those stones are still around since 3500 years and they still will be around in the future, when our bodies might be buried underneath a stone. F — Flint. Fossile. Forever. Enter deep time. Time of geological layers or oceans where there are mountains now. Uncovering ancient fossils. An anthology of grave stones. The landscape sleeping, all the plants dead but still alive. The sun just about to reaching towards us. The longest night of the year. We ventured to greet the first stones, just resting in between some trees. Wands out, our hands caressing the cold and abrasive surface. A sanding sensual tickling. We followed a narrow path through the empty sleeping landscape of German winter. And there they were resting. Two big stones broken in half, like an egg. Stone eggs. Nestled in between naked trees. We lay down, listening with our ears for secret whisperings. Tuning in with our own bones, resonating and vibrating with the minerals of our human bodies. Our restless brains and meat suits. Finding fantasy forms and language for them. Some people might pass by and just see a random collection of big rocks. G — Grave. Granit. Glimpses. What is a crypt? A hidden vault built of stone. The word derives from the Greek krypte – a vault, and kryptos – hidden. Stones have been used forever in human time to enclose the dead. Structures hidden in the earth. Marking spaces of burial. A warning? A remembrance? A sacred site? A renewal of becoming earth again. The psychoanalytical crypt is a tomb inside the psyche. Inaccessible. Without language. It contains silences, secrets, a loss that could not be mourned. As an attempt to keep on living, something had to be split and closed away. The self: a cemetery guard. Prohibiting access to a foreign place without even knowing what it is guarding. Turning visitors away. Offering false leads. Fake graves. There can be collective crypts, communal crypts, national crypts. The Germans buried what they had lost yet denied having lost – not even the people, the lovers, the friends, the places, the communities, but also their own humanity. A heavy gravestone of a nameless grave that has to be carried around. I imagine him enclosed in a stone suit. H — Hidden. History. Höhle. Humus. Hole. Myth. A stone wall. A hand on moss. A secret entrance into a hill, a mound. Someone built a cave in the ground. Will you enter? I am scared of caves, narrow passages in the earth. But there is an intense attraction. A black hole that somehow draws you closer, whispers to you. Come enter me. Come into me. The womb of the earth. Only to discover there is a whole dark universe inside. I like to linger in the transition zone, the entrance portal. A little wizard with a wand. A wooden spiral wand touching a stone. Here lies ________ who could not be mourned. I — Inschrift. He spoke in riddles to me. It felt like there was something I inherited. Some kind of crypt. Some kind of silence. Some kind of stone in my heart. A heaviness, some kind of condensed, layered piece of rock that I am still trying to decipher. J — Jasper. D's paintings speak to me about humans and stones, where the borders get thin. Where humans become stones and stones become human. The thin transformation zone between stones and bodies. Stone beings. Stones becoming alive. Bodies mixing with stones. Pulling things out of rubble. Discovery of death. Preserved life. K — Knochen. Kalkstein. Kiesel. What is there to say. There was a concentration camp in Dachau. And there was a silence. A stone in my heart is also a seed. A pit. Hard, dense, condensed. Waiting. L — Lava. Landscape. Layers. M — Mine. Moor. Marble. Minerals. Movement. Something that has no language. Transgenerational tombs, inheritances of violent histories, of boreholes in the earth. Wounds that have been torn into the earth body. Exctraction of fossils, fueling technological ideas. Human hands, someone’s hands. A loss that is unimaginable, hard to conceive. Unbearable weights balanced on precarious structures that brush soft human bodies. If felt, if not they would be hidden away someplace else. N — Narbe. A tender spot. Ein Kleiner Hügel, eine Gewebeerhebung. Evidence of healing powers. Proof of an injury. O — Obsidian. What was hidden is unknown. Maybe it has already decomposed. We linger at the entrance, maybe searching for a way in. Or maybe not, do we need to know see the remains. Or is someone inhabitating this place. Something is trapped within the psyche. Something that one could or would not bury, not mourn in reality. Not even acknowledge its loss. Derrida describes the self as a cemetery guard prohibiting access to a foreign place in the psyche without even knowing what it is guarding. P — Pit. Q — Quarz. pit (n.) — Old English pytt. A natural depression in the ground. Water hole. Well. Grave. A pit is also the hard seed inside a fruit. The condensed potentiality of life. Hard like stone. Yet it cracks open. My friend collects pits. Seeds. They are hard like stones. Where is the border between what is alive and what is dead? Mosses grow on stones. Lichens. Water and stone is where something grows. What is hidden inside that creates life? pith (n.) — Old English piþa. Essential part. Quintessence. Condensed substance. R — Rätsel. Rubble. Ruin. What is a riddle? A puzzle. A thing that is hard to understand at first glance. Something lies hidden. The answer rests within the space between words, embedded underneath the layers, enclosed and then extracted for the sophisticated ones. It is also a coarse sieve for grains or sand – to riddle out the stones. To riddle means to perforate something with many holes. Language with holes. Meaning with holes. My father loved riddles. So he became one. S — Sediment. Siegel. Sandstein. Stein. Stone. Spirit. What is a stone? A piece of earth, of rock, detached from its source. A hard mass, concentrated sedimented layers of history. Ancient as geological time – time that no human can understand. A stone is a riddle. Stein (n.) — eng verbunden mit der indogermanischen Wurzel stāi−/stī−: fest werden. Gerinnen. Verdichten. To become stone. To solidify. To condense. To close. My father loved stones. So he became one. Hard and rough and out of place. Lying around. T — Time. Tomb. Transformation. Memento mori. Alea iacta est. Vale dulcis anima. Once we visited a dolmen next to a parking lot. It was so out of place, people passing by, cars, noise. The stones just resting, the landscape around them completely changed. They've been there so long. They will be there long after. U — Untergrund. Abraham and Torok describe a psychoanalytical understanding of the crypt, as a psychic tomb that is hidden, a place inaccessible with words, as it contains silences, secrets. It often is connected to a traumatic loss of something that could not be mourned. As an attempt to bury something to keep on living it had to be split and closed away. Often it conceals a trauma which emotional consequence would have been to dangerous. Some grand loss of sort that could not be mourned. Gabriela Schwab writes about the concept of collective crypt, of communal or even national crypts, where she draws the connection to Alexander and margarete Mitscherlich who wrote about the German inability to mourn after World War II instead or mourning what has been lost, not even the people, lovers, friends, places, communities but also their own humanity this loss had to be denied and buried deep within a psychic tomb. Something that was silenced, a collective silencing that gets passed on to next generations, something that is heavy, weighing one down, a heavy gravestone of a nameless grave that has to be carried around. V — Versteinerung. A tomb in the psychic landscape. What was hidden is unknown. Maybe it has already decomposed. We linger at the entrance, maybe searching for a way in. Or maybe not. Do we need to see the remains. Or is someone still inhabiting this place. W — Well. Within. What gets excavated and what lies hidden. crypt (n.) — early 15c., from Latin crypta, from Greek krypte, short for krypte kamara: hidden vault. From kryptein: to hide. enigma (n.) — 1530s, from Greek ainigma: a dark saying. From ainissesthai: to speak obscurely, to speak in riddles. How deep is it? X — _________ Y — _________ Z — Zeit. Zerfall. Zone. We are circling around something that lies hidden within geological layers. Those stones are still around since 3500 years. They still will be around in the future, when our bodies might be buried underneath a stone.
